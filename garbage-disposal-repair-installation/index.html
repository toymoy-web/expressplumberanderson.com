Jam Or Failure

Diagnosing A Disposal That Won't Run

A disposal that hums but won't spin is almost always jammed, usually by a utensil, fruit pit, or fibrous food that's wedged the flywheel. That's a repair most homeowners can attempt safely with the power off using the reset button and the hex-key wrench opening on the bottom of most units, though a jam that won't clear that way needs the unit opened up.

A disposal that's silent and unresponsive is a different problem, usually a tripped reset button, a bad connection at the switch, or a failed motor on an older unit that's simply reached the end of its service life.

Signs & Symptoms

Repair Or Replace

When Replacement Makes More Sense

A jam or a tripped reset is a repair, usually resolved in the same visit. A disposal leaking from the bottom housing, rather than a fitting, is leaking from the motor seal itself, which isn't a repairable part on most residential units and points to replacement.

For a new install, correct sizing matters more than most homeowners expect: a unit sized too small for household cooking volume jams more often and burns out sooner, while an oversized unit for a light-use kitchen is simply an unnecessary cost.

What Not To Put Down A Disposal

Habits That Cause The Most Jams

Grease and cooking oil solidify inside the disposal and the drain line below it, which is the single most common cause of both jams and the recurring kitchen clogs that follow. Fibrous vegetables like celery, corn husks, and onion skins wrap around the flywheel rather than grinding cleanly, which is the second most common jam cause we see on service calls.

Running cold water, not hot, while the disposal operates keeps grease solid enough to grind and flush through rather than melting and coating the line further down.

Ice cubes run through periodically help knock loose buildup on the grinding components, and a small amount of dish soap run through afterward helps clear lingering odor without resorting to harsh chemical drain cleaners that can damage the disposal's internal seals over time. New installation follows the Indiana Plumbing Code standard (675 IAC 16) for the electrical and drain connections involved.
