Catching It Before It Bursts

Frozen Doesn't Always Mean Broken Yet

A pipe that's frozen but hasn't split yet is still a repairable situation, not necessarily a flood in progress. No water from a fixture during a hard freeze, especially one that previously worked fine, is the clearest early sign, and getting it thawed safely before ice fully expands the pipe wall can prevent the burst entirely.

Anderson sees genuine hard freezes most winters, and the pipes most at risk are the ones in unheated or marginally heated spaces, an uninsulated crawlspace, an exterior wall, or a garage, rather than anything in a consistently heated living space.

Signs & Symptoms

Signs A Pipe Has Frozen

No Water From A Fixture

The most common first sign, especially during or right after a hard freeze.

Frost Visible On An Exposed Pipe

A visible sign on pipes in a crawlspace, garage, or basement.

Strange Smell From A Faucet Or Drain

Can indicate a frozen vent pipe forcing sewer gas back through fixtures.

Bulging Section Of Pipe

A pipe visibly swollen from internal ice is close to splitting, if it hasn't already.

Method

Safe Thawing Vs. DIY Risk

Safe thawing uses controlled heat, an electric heat tape, a heat gun kept moving, or hot air directed at the frozen section, working from the faucet end back toward the blockage so melting water has somewhere to go. An open flame or a method that heats one spot too fast can crack an already-stressed pipe or start a fire in wall cavities and framing, which is the real risk with common DIY thawing attempts.

If the pipe has already split, thawing it will start an active leak immediately, so we shut off water to that section first and plan the repair, using materials and methods that meet the Indiana Plumbing Code standard (675 IAC 16), before applying heat.

What To Do Before We Arrive

  • Locate and be ready to access your main water shutoff valve.
  • Open a nearby faucet slightly to relieve pressure once thawing begins.
  • Do not use an open flame or torch anywhere near the frozen section.
Prevention

What Actually Prevents A Repeat Freeze

Pipe insulation sleeves on exposed runs, sealing drafts where a pipe passes through an exterior wall or foundation, and keeping cabinet doors open under sinks on exterior walls during a hard freeze are the three most effective, lowest-cost prevention steps for the pattern we see most often in Anderson.

For a crawlspace with a history of freezing pipes, insulating the crawlspace itself rather than just the individual pipes addresses the actual temperature problem instead of one symptom of it.

Letting a faucet drip slightly on the coldest nights relieves pressure inside the line, which can be the difference between a pipe that survives a hard freeze intact and one that splits even with insulation in place.
