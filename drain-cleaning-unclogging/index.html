Clog Or Something Bigger

When A Slow Drain Means More Than One Clog

A single slow drain is usually a local clog right at that fixture. When multiple drains back up at once, or the same drain clogs again within weeks of being cleared, that pattern points to the main line rather than the fixture itself, especially in the older housing near downtown Anderson where cast iron and clay main lines are common.

We snake the fixture first for a straightforward local clog, and move to a camera inspection or hydro jetting when the pattern suggests root intrusion or grease buildup further down the line rather than a one-time blockage.

Method

Snaking, Camera Inspection, And Hydro Jetting

A standard drain snake clears the immediate blockage at the fixture or in the accessible run of pipe. When a clog keeps recurring, a camera inspection shows exactly what is happening inside the line, whether that is root intrusion, grease buildup, or a section of pipe that has settled and now traps debris.

Hydro jetting uses high-pressure water to scour the full interior diameter of the pipe rather than punching a hole through the clog, which is the better fix for grease buildup or a line that clogs on a predictable schedule.

Prevention

What Actually Prevents The Next Clog

Grease poured down a kitchen drain is solid at room temperature, and it coats the inside of the pipe further down the line than most homeowners assume, well past the trap under the sink. That coating is what most kitchen-line hydro jetting jobs are clearing, not a single blockage at one point. A jammed or leaking garbage disposal is a separate but frequently related cause of a kitchen drain backing up.

For main lines running under mature trees, which describes a lot of the older lots near the White River corridor and downtown Anderson, root intrusion through pipe joints is a recurring cause rather than a one-time event. A camera inspection every couple of years catches root growth before it causes a backup.

Bathroom drains tend to clog with hair and soap residue rather than grease, which is a different problem with a different fix: a mechanical snake handles it well, and a drain strainer at the fixture prevents most of it from happening again in the first place. If the toilet itself is the actual source of a bathroom backup rather than the drain line, our toilet repair and installation page covers that separately.
