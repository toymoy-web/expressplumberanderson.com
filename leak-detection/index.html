The Diagnostic-First Approach

Finding The Leak Before Touching Anything

Most leak calls start the same way: a water bill that jumped for no obvious reason, a damp or warm spot on the floor, or the sound of running water with no fixture actually running. None of those tell you where the leak actually is, which is why the first step is always locating it electronically rather than opening walls or flooring on a guess.

We use acoustic listening equipment and, for slab leaks, thermal imaging to pinpoint the leak's location within a foot or two before any repair work starts, so the only opening made is the one that actually reaches the pipe.

Signs & Symptoms

Signs You Have A Hidden Leak

Water Bill Spiked

A jump with no change in household water use usually means a leak, often at the meter side.

Meter Still Running With Everything Off

Shut off every fixture and check the meter; if it's still moving, water is escaping somewhere.

Warm Spot On The Floor

Often a sign of a hot-water line leaking under a slab.

Sound Of Running Water

Audible water movement with no fixture running points to a supply-line leak.

Equipment

How Electronic Leak Tracing Works

Acoustic leak detection equipment listens for the specific sound water makes escaping a pressurized line, which travels along the pipe and can be traced from accessible points like a meter box or a basement wall. For a suspected slab leak, thermal imaging can show the warm signature of a hot-water line leaking beneath the concrete.

Once the location is confirmed, repair options range from a direct spot repair to rerouting the line entirely, and we walk through the honest trade-offs of each before any concrete or drywall comes out.

Insurance & Documentation

What To Keep For A Claim

A sudden, accidental leak, like a supply line that bursts, is typically covered under a standard homeowner's policy, while gradual leaks that developed over months are more often excluded as a maintenance issue rather than a covered event. That distinction is why documenting when you first noticed the problem matters as much as documenting the damage itself.

We provide a written diagnostic report showing where the leak was located and what caused it, which most insurers want alongside photos of the damage before approving a claim. Keep that report along with any receipts for the detection visit itself, since some policies reimburse the diagnostic cost when the repair is covered.
