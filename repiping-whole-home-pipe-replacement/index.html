When Repeated Leaks Mean Repipe

The Point Where Patching Stops Making Sense

Repiping replaces the interior supply lines throughout the house, rather than fixing one leak at a time. It's the right call when galvanized steel pipe, common in Anderson homes built before the 1960s near downtown and the White River corridor, has corroded from the inside to the point where leaks keep appearing at new spots even after each one gets patched.

Galvanized pipe corrodes from the inside out, narrowing the interior diameter and reducing water pressure years before it actually starts leaking, so reduced flow throughout the house is often the first sign, well before any visible leak.

Materials & Approach

PEX Vs. Copper For A Repipe

Copper

  • Long, proven track record
  • Rigid, doesn't flex through tight framing
  • Higher material cost
  • More joints, more potential leak points

PEX

  • Flexible, fewer fittings needed
  • Faster install, less drywall disruption
  • Resistant to the corrosion that failed the old galvanized pipe
  • Lower material and labor cost overall
Permits & Timeline

What A Repipe Project Actually Involves

A whole-home repipe requires a permit and inspection under the Indiana Plumbing Code standard (675 IAC 16), since it's a substantial alteration to the home's supply system rather than routine repair. Water is shut off in sections rather than for the entire duration of the project on most jobs, so the home isn't fully without water the whole time.

We coordinate the inspection and provide a written scope before starting, so you know which walls will be opened and what the drywall patching will involve before committing to the project.

Homes with a finished basement or slab-on-grade construction sometimes need a different repipe approach than a home with an open crawlspace, since the available access points change which walls, if any, actually need to be opened. A pre-repipe walkthrough identifies which approach fits your specific layout before any wall is opened.
