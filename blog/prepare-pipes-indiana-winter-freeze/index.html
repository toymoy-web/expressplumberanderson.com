How To Prepare Your Pipes For An Indiana Winter

Indiana's hard freezes are real and predictable. These are the specific steps that actually reduce frozen and burst pipe risk, not generic advice.

A hard freeze in central Indiana isn't a rare event, it's a predictable part of most winters, and the pipes that fail are almost always the same category: exposed or poorly insulated runs in unheated or marginally heated spaces. An uninsulated crawlspace, an exterior wall, a garage, or an under-sink cabinet against an outside wall are the specific locations worth checking before the season's first hard freeze, not the whole house generally.

The Three Steps That Actually Matter

Insulation, Draft Sealing, And Pressure Relief

Pipe insulation sleeves on exposed runs are inexpensive and directly address the problem: they slow heat loss from the pipe to the surrounding cold air. Sealing drafts where a pipe passes through an exterior wall or foundation matters just as much, since a well-insulated pipe still freezes if cold outside air is blowing directly across that insulation through a gap.

On the coldest nights, letting a faucet drip slightly relieves pressure inside the line. Water expands as it freezes; a completely closed system with no give anywhere has nowhere for that expansion to go except splitting the pipe wall. A slow drip keeps water moving just enough to reduce that risk, even in a marginally insulated section.

Older Homes Need More Attention

Why This Matters More In Established Neighborhoods

Homes built before the 1970s in Anderson and in older towns like Elwood, where much of the housing dates to before World War II, often have less wall insulation around plumbing than current construction requires. That's not a flaw specific to any one house, it reflects the building standards of the era, but it does mean these homes benefit more from proactive pipe insulation than a newly built home with modern wall assemblies.

If a specific section of pipe has frozen before, insulating just that section again for this winter isn't enough on its own, the underlying cold-air exposure that caused it the first time is usually still there unless something changed.

Before The First Hard Freeze

  • Insulate exposed pipes in crawlspaces, garages, and under exterior-wall sinks.
  • Seal visible gaps where pipes pass through exterior walls or the foundation.
  • Know where your main water shutoff valve is, in case a pipe does split.
  • Open cabinet doors under exterior-wall sinks during the coldest nights.
If A Pipe Freezes Anyway

What To Do Before It Splits

No water from a fixture during a hard freeze is usually the first sign, and it's still a repairable situation at that point, not necessarily a flood in progress. Our frozen pipe repair uses safe thawing that works from the open end of the pipe back toward the frozen section using controlled heat, never an open flame, which can crack an already-stressed pipe or ignite nearby framing. If the pipe has already split, that's a different, faster-moving situation covered by our burst pipe repair service instead.

Outdoor Fixtures Too

Don't Forget Hose Bibs And Outdoor Spigots

Outdoor faucets are exposed directly to the cold with only a thin wall section separating them from the interior supply line, which makes them one of the most common freeze points on the whole house. Disconnecting garden hoses before the first hard freeze and, where installed, closing the interior shutoff valve for an outdoor spigot and draining the remaining water are both quick steps that meaningfully cut the risk.

A frost-free hose bib, common on newer construction, is designed to shut off water further back inside the heated wall cavity, but even those can fail if a hose is left attached, since the attached hose traps water in the section that's supposed to drain.

If You're Leaving Town

Preparing A Home For An Extended Winter Absence

Beyond keeping the thermostat at a safe minimum, shutting off the main water valve entirely before an extended trip removes the risk altogether for the interior supply, provided someone can check on the house periodically to make sure nothing else needs attention. For a water heater, this is also a reasonable time to check that the temperature and pressure relief valve isn't showing signs of dripping, since a small leak left unattended for weeks can cause real water damage.

Asking a neighbor or family member to check the house during a cold snap while you're away is a simple step that catches a developing problem, frozen pipe or otherwise, before it becomes a much larger one by the time you return. It costs nothing beyond a phone call, and it's the single easiest prevention step on this entire list to actually follow through on.
