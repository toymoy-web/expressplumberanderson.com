Why This Matters

Protecting The Drinking Water Supply

A backflow preventer stops contaminated water from being pulled backward into the clean drinking water supply during a pressure drop, such as a water main break or heavy firefighting draw nearby. It's required under the Indiana Plumbing Code standard (675 IAC 16) on irrigation systems, commercial properties, and any connection where a cross-contamination risk exists, and most jurisdictions, including Anderson, require annual testing to confirm the device is still functioning correctly.

A failed test isn't just a compliance problem, it means the device genuinely isn't protecting the water supply the way it's supposed to, which is worth treating as a real repair priority rather than paperwork.

Testing Process

What A Certified Test Involves

Testing uses calibrated gauges to confirm the device's check valves hold pressure correctly in both directions, and results are documented on the form most water utilities and jurisdictions require to be filed after testing. If a device fails, we can often repair or rebuild the internal components on the same visit rather than requiring a full device replacement.

Compliance

Why Testing Documentation Matters

Filed test results are typically required by the water utility or local jurisdiction as proof of an active, functioning backflow prevention device, and missing that filing can result in a compliance notice even if the device itself is working fine. We handle filing the required paperwork after testing so that step doesn't fall on the property owner to track down separately.

Commercial properties often have multiple backflow devices across a single site: one at the domestic water entry, another on a fire sprinkler line, and sometimes a third on an irrigation connection. Each one requires its own annual test and its own filed paperwork rather than a single blanket certification for the property.
