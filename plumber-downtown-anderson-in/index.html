About Downtown

Anderson's Historic Downtown

Downtown Anderson is the city's central business district, and its core is a National Register historic district. The district runs roughly along Meridian Street from 10th Street to the former Conrail rail line, plus the first block west of 11th and 12th Streets. It covers about 17 acres and was listed on April 19, 2006.

The district has 32 contributing buildings from about 1887 to 1955, in styles such as Italianate and Classical Revival. The Paramount Theatre and the Anderson Center for the Arts are among its best-known buildings. Downtown also includes apartment housing, such as the historic Tower, built in 1926.

Mixed-Use Plumbing

Businesses, Offices And Apartments

This page is about a commercial core, not a residential subdivision. For houses near downtown, see our pages for West Central and West Eighth Street.

Downtown buildings often mix uses under one roof, with a shop or restaurant on the ground floor and offices or apartments above. Shared drain lines and shared water service can make a problem on one floor show up on another. We start by finding out which spaces share which lines.

Restaurant And Cafe Drains

Kitchens that handle grease need regular drain care. Hydro jetting clears buildup that a standard snake leaves behind.

Restrooms And Fixtures

Public restrooms see heavy use. Quick fixture repairs keep a business open and avoid water damage.

Backflow Protection

Some commercial water connections need a backflow device that is tested on a schedule. Rules vary by building and utility.

After-Hours Calls

When A Space Changes Hands

Tenant Turnover Is A Good Time To Look

Before a new restaurant, salon or office fits out a downtown space, it helps to confirm what is already there. That means which fixtures and drain lines exist, where the shutoffs are, and whether the water heater suits the new use. A short walk-through can prevent surprises after the build-out.

If a drain has a history of slow flow, a camera inspection shows what is happening inside the line before walls and floors are finished. Our commercial plumbing page covers new construction rough-in and tenant improvements as well.

If You Notice A Leak Or Backup In A Building

  • Shut off the water at the unit or the building main. Know where both are before you need them.
  • Keep people away from wet outlets and electrical equipment.
  • Move stock, records and furnishings out of the wet area if it is safe.
  • Call us, and tell the building manager so shared lines can be checked.
