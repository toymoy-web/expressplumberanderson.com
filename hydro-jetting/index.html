When Snaking Isn't Enough

The Difference Between Punching Through And Clearing Out

A standard drain snake punches a hole through whatever is blocking the line, which restores flow immediately but leaves the rest of the buildup coating the pipe walls untouched. On a kitchen line with years of grease buildup, or a main line with root intrusion at the joints, that leftover coating is exactly what causes the same clog to come back within weeks.

Hydro jetting uses a high-pressure water stream, run through a specialized nozzle, to scour the full interior diameter of the pipe rather than drilling a path through the middle. We run a camera inspection first on any line with a recurring pattern, since jetting a line with a genuine structural crack instead of buildup can make a bad section worse.

Process

Camera First, Then Jetting

We run a sewer camera through the line before jetting on any recurring-clog call, which shows whether the problem is buildup that jetting will resolve or a structural issue like a bellied or cracked section that jetting alone won't fix. Jetting pressure is also adjusted for pipe material and age, since older clay or thin-wall cast iron needs a gentler setting than modern PVC.

After jetting, we run the camera again to confirm the line is genuinely clear, not just flowing well enough to pass a quick water test.

Before A Jetting Appointment

  • Clear the area around any cleanout access point in your yard or basement.
  • Avoid running water or using fixtures tied to the affected line right before we arrive.
  • Let us know if you know the pipe material or the home's approximate age.
Maintenance Scheduling

How Often A Line Actually Needs Jetting

A line with a known root intrusion problem often benefits from jetting on a preventive schedule, roughly every 12 to 18 months, rather than waiting for the next backup. That schedule is cheaper over several years than repeated emergency calls plus the water damage cleanup that can follow a backup inside the house.

A kitchen line without a known structural issue usually doesn't need routine jetting at all if grease disposal habits change, which is often the more cost-effective fix than jetting the same line every year indefinitely.

Commercial kitchens are a different case, since grease accumulates far faster under regular cooking volume, and a scheduled jetting service is usually written into a commercial lease or health code requirement rather than optional maintenance.
