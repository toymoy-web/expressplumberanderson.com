Growth Along Fall Creek

Plumbing In A Fast-Growing Town

Pendleton has grown by nearly half since 2000. Its median home value has climbed 86% since 2011 alone. Both are real signs of a town filling in with new construction rather than aging in place. That growth means most Pendleton plumbing calls we get involve new-construction fixture work: sizing a water heater or softener correctly for a larger household, rather than fixing the failing galvanized pipe common in Anderson's older core.

Pendleton's older section sits near downtown and Falls Park, where Fall Creek drops over a natural falls. It still has some original mid-century housing worth checking for aging supply lines. But it's a smaller share of the town's housing stock than in Anderson's established neighborhoods.

The town platted in 1830 sits along the Fall Creek corridor and has become a genuine Indianapolis-metro commuter community, with Pendleton Heights High School serving the growing school-age population that's come with it. That commuter growth is exactly why new-construction plumbing work now outpaces aging-infrastructure repair here.
