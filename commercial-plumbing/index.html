Different Standards, Different Stakes

Why Commercial Plumbing Isn't Just Bigger Residential Work

Commercial plumbing operates under different code requirements than residential work, particularly around backflow prevention, grease interceptor sizing for food service, and fixture counts tied to occupancy load under the Indiana Plumbing Code. A commercial property also usually can't tolerate downtime the way a residence can, so scheduling around business hours matters as much as the repair itself.

We work with property managers, restaurant operators, and office tenants across Anderson on everything from a single leaking fixture to full plumbing scope on a new build-out or tenant improvement.

Approach

Scheduling Around Your Business

Most commercial work can be scheduled after hours or during a slower business period specifically to avoid disrupting customers or operations, and we coordinate directly with property management or ownership on timing rather than defaulting to standard daytime scheduling.

For a new build-out or tenant improvement, we work from the architect or engineer's plans and pull the required commercial permit, which typically involves a more detailed plan review than residential permitting.

Compliance

What Commercial Properties Are Usually Required To Maintain

Beyond backflow testing, many commercial properties in Madison County are subject to periodic fixture and fire-suppression-adjacent plumbing inspections depending on occupancy type, which is worth confirming with the local building department for your specific property class rather than assuming residential rules apply.

ADA-compliant fixture counts and clearances are another commercial-specific requirement that doesn't come up in residential work, particularly relevant on any tenant improvement or new build-out that includes public restrooms.

We keep a record of prior service work at a given property, which speeds up diagnosis on repeat calls and helps property managers track maintenance history across a portfolio rather than starting from scratch with each new issue.

For multi-tenant buildings, isolating which unit a plumbing issue actually originates from before opening any wall saves real time and cost, since a leak that shows up in one tenant's ceiling often traces back to a fixture in a completely different unit above it.
