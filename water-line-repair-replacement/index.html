A Different Line Than What's Inside

What The Water Service Line Actually Covers

The water service line runs underground from the Anderson Utilities meter or curb stop to the point it enters the house, separate from the interior supply piping a repipe addresses. A leak on this line often shows up as a wet spot in the yard between the meter and the house, a hissing sound near the meter box, or a water bill that's risen with no change in household usage.

Older service lines in Anderson's established neighborhoods are sometimes still galvanized steel or, in some older sections, lead, both of which are candidates for replacement regardless of whether an active leak has developed yet.

Repair Options

Trenchless Replacement Vs. Open Trench

Trenchless water line replacement pulls new pipe through the path of the old line using directional boring or pipe bursting, avoiding a continuous open trench across the yard. It's our first option to evaluate on most Anderson lots, though a line running under a driveway, mature landscaping, or another utility crossing sometimes still calls for open-trench access.

A localized leak on an otherwise sound line can sometimes be spot-repaired rather than replacing the full run, which we determine after locating the exact leak point, following the Indiana Plumbing Code standard (675 IAC 16) for the materials and fittings used.

Before We Arrive

  • Locate your main shutoff valve in case water needs to be cut off during the visit.
  • Note when you first noticed the leak, low pressure, or high bill.
  • Clear access to the meter box and any visible wet area in the yard.
Lead Line Considerations

Why Older Service Lines Are Worth Replacing Proactively

A lead service line poses a genuine health consideration beyond just leak risk, since lead can leach into drinking water, particularly if the water sits in the line for extended periods. Replacing a known or suspected lead service line is worth doing proactively rather than waiting for it to fail, even without an active leak forcing the decision.

A new or replaced service line connection is also the point where a backflow prevention device is typically installed if one is required for your property, since it protects the drinking water supply right at the point where the service line enters the home.
