A Small Leak, A Real Cost

Why A Dripping Faucet Is Worth Fixing Promptly

A faucet dripping once every few seconds sounds minor, but it adds up to thousands of gallons a year, which shows up on the water bill long before most homeowners connect the two. Most drips trace back to a worn cartridge, O-ring, or washer inside the faucet body, a straightforward repair on nearly every residential fixture.

A leak at the supply line or shutoff valve under the sink is a different problem, usually a failed compression fitting or a valve that has corroded after years of exposure to Anderson's harder municipal water, and it can cause real cabinet and flooring damage if it goes unnoticed. If a bathroom project involves more than the faucet itself, our shower and bathtub installation page covers full fixture replacement separately.

Repair Approach

Cartridge Repair Vs. Full Replacement

Most dripping faucets are repaired by replacing the internal cartridge or washer rather than the whole fixture, which is faster and considerably less expensive. We keep common cartridges for major brands stocked on the truck, though a less common or discontinued model sometimes needs a specific part ordered.

Full replacement makes more sense for a faucet that has failed repeatedly, shows visible corrosion at the base, or simply doesn't match a kitchen or bathroom remodel anymore.

Prevention

What Extends A Faucet's Working Life

Anderson's harder municipal water accelerates wear on the rubber and plastic components inside a faucet cartridge, which is a big part of why repair calls here trend earlier than in a soft-water market. A whole-home water softener slows that wear across every fixture in the house, not just one faucet at a time.

Cleaning the aerator every few months, unscrewing the tip and rinsing out mineral buildup, is a simple step that keeps water pressure consistent and reduces strain on the internal cartridge from debris getting trapped against it.

A pull-down kitchen faucet sprayer that drips or won't retract properly is a related but distinct repair, usually a worn diverter valve or hose weight, and we carry common replacement parts for most major kitchen faucet brands on the truck. All fixture replacement work follows the Indiana Plumbing Code standard (675 IAC 16) for supply connections and shutoff valve requirements.
