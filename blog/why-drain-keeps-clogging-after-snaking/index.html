Why Does My Drain Keep Clogging After I Snake It?

If the same drain backs up again within a few weeks of clearing it, the snake cleared the symptom, not the cause. Here's the actual difference.

A drain snake works by punching a path through whatever is blocking the pipe, a rotating cable that breaks apart or pushes past the clog to restore flow. That's genuinely effective for a one-time blockage: a foreign object, a hair clump, a sudden buildup that hasn't accumulated over years. It does not clean the rest of the pipe's interior surface, which is exactly why the same drain can clog again within weeks if the underlying cause is buildup rather than a single blockage.

The Real Difference

Punching Through Vs. Cleaning Out

Grease is the clearest example. Cooking grease is liquid when hot and solidifies as it cools inside the pipe, coating the interior walls well past the trap under a kitchen sink. A snake punches through the center of that coating, restoring flow immediately, but leaves the grease lining the pipe walls exactly where it was. Within weeks, that lining catches new debris and narrows back down to a clog.

Root intrusion on a main line follows a similar pattern from a different cause. Roots seeking moisture work into pipe joints and grow inward over years; snaking clears the immediate blockage the roots created, but the roots themselves remain in the joint, ready to regrow into the cleared path.

The Actual Fix

When Hydro Jetting Is Worth It

Hydro jetting uses high-pressure water through a specialized nozzle to scour the full interior diameter of the pipe, not just punch a path through the middle. On a grease-lined kitchen line or a main line with root intrusion at multiple joints, that's the difference between a fix that lasts and one that buys a few more weeks.

A camera inspection before jetting confirms what's actually causing the recurrence, buildup that jetting will resolve, or a structural issue like a cracked or bellied section that jetting can't fix on its own. Jetting a structurally damaged line without knowing that first can make a bad section worse.

Signs You Need More Than Another Snake

  • The same drain has clogged more than once in the last two months.
  • Multiple fixtures back up together, not just one isolated drain.
  • You've noticed grease or cooking oil regularly going down a kitchen drain.
Older Homes, Older Lines

Why This Comes Up More In Established Neighborhoods

Homes with original clay or cast iron main lines, common in Anderson's older neighborhoods and in towns like Alexandria where housing predates the 1950s, see this pattern more often than newer PVC lines. Clay pipe joints are a real, checkable point where root intrusion recurs even after a line has been cleared multiple times, which is why a homeowner in an older house who's snaked the same drain three times this year is dealing with a structural pattern, not bad luck.

What Not To Do

Chemical Drain Cleaners Aren't The Answer Either

Chemical drain cleaners work similarly to a snake in one important way: they clear the immediate blockage without addressing buildup along the pipe walls, and they carry a real downside a snake doesn't. According to most plumbing manufacturers' own guidance, repeated use can corrode older metal pipes and degrade rubber seals and gaskets over time, which turns a clog problem into a leak problem on top of it.

If a drain has already resisted one round of chemical treatment, running a second dose rarely succeeds where the first one failed, and it adds more corrosive exposure to a pipe that may already be older and more vulnerable than a newer PVC line would be.

A Simple Prevention Habit

What Actually Reduces Future Clogs

A drain strainer at the kitchen sink or shower drain catches food scraps and hair before they enter the pipe at all, which is a genuinely low-cost step compared to any repeat service call. For kitchen drains specifically, letting grease cool and disposing of it in the trash rather than the sink prevents the single most common cause of recurring kitchen-line clogs we see.

Neither habit fixes buildup that's already accumulated in an older line, but both meaningfully slow how quickly a newly cleared line clogs again, which stretches the time between hydro jetting appointments even on a line with a known history. Combined with an occasional preventive camera check on a line with a known root problem, these small habits add up to real savings over several years, at essentially no cost beyond a small change in daily kitchen routine.
