Why Is My Water Heater Making Noise?

Different sounds point to different causes. Here's how to tell a routine maintenance issue from a bigger problem, just by listening.

A water heater is supposed to run quietly, so any new or unusual sound is worth paying attention to, though not every sound means the same thing or requires the same urgency. Matching the specific sound to its likely cause is a genuinely useful first step before deciding whether this is a call-today situation or something that can wait for a scheduled visit.

Popping And Rumbling

The Most Common Water Heater Sound

A popping or rumbling sound, especially during the heating cycle, almost always means sediment has settled at the bottom of the tank. As the burner or heating element heats water trapped beneath that sediment layer, steam bubbles form and escape through the sediment, creating the popping sound. This is common on Anderson's harder municipal water, where mineral sediment accumulates faster than in a soft-water market, and it's more of a maintenance issue than an emergency, though it does reduce efficiency and can shorten the tank's working life if left unaddressed. Manufacturer maintenance standards for most residential tank units specify an annual flush partly to prevent exactly this kind of buildup.

Before You Call About A Water Heater Sound

  • Note when the sound happens: during heating, right after use, or constantly.
  • Check for any visible water at the base of the tank.
  • Note whether the sound is new or has been present for a while, gradually worsening.
Ticking Or Tapping

Often Just Normal Expansion

A soft ticking or tapping sound, particularly right after hot water use, is frequently just the pipes expanding and contracting slightly with temperature change, which is normal and not usually a cause for concern on its own. If the ticking is loud, persistent, or accompanied by other symptoms, it's worth mentioning during a service visit, but in isolation this is one of the less urgent sounds on this list.

Knocking Or Banging

Water Hammer, Not The Tank Itself

A sharp knocking or banging sound, especially right when a valve closes, is often water hammer, a pressure wave caused by water suddenly stopping in the pipe, rather than a problem with the water heater itself. This can be addressed with a water hammer arrestor installed at the affected fixture and doesn't necessarily indicate anything wrong with the heater, though it's worth confirming that's actually the cause rather than assuming.

Sounds Worth A Same-Day Call

  • A hissing sound suggesting an active leak or escaping pressure.
  • Any sound accompanied by visible water at the base of the tank.
  • A loud, sudden bang rather than a repeated knocking pattern.
  • Any burning smell alongside an unusual sound.
Hissing Or Screeching

A Sound Worth Acting On Promptly

A hissing sound can indicate the temperature and pressure relief valve is releasing pressure, which is the valve doing its job in response to excess pressure, but it's still worth having checked promptly, since it means something upstream is causing that excess pressure to build in the first place. A screeching sound during water flow sometimes points to a partially closed valve somewhere in the system restricting flow at an unusual angle.

Age Also Matters For Interpreting Sounds

Why The Same Sound Means Different Things On An Older Unit

A rumbling sound on a unit under 5 years old usually means simple sediment buildup that a flush resolves cleanly. The same rumbling on a unit past 10 years old can indicate more advanced internal corrosion alongside the sediment, in which case a flush still helps but may not fully quiet the unit the way it would on a younger tank. That distinction is worth raising during a service call, since it changes whether a flush alone is likely to be a lasting fix or a temporary improvement.

When Sediment Is The Cause

Flushing Vs. Living With It

If popping and rumbling are the sounds you're hearing, a full tank flush removes the sediment causing them, which is a routine part of our water heater repair and maintenance service. Left unaddressed indefinitely, sediment buildup insulates the heating element from the water, forcing the unit to work harder and shortening its life beyond just being noisy. Pairing a flush with a water softener installation reduces how quickly sediment reaccumulates afterward, which matters more in a hard-water market like Anderson than it would elsewhere.

This pattern shows up across the whole service area, including newer construction in Pendleton, where even a relatively new water heater can develop noticeable sediment noise within a few years given the local water hardness, regardless of how new the unit itself is. A whole-home softener slows this pattern considerably, which is worth discussing during any flush appointment rather than treating each noise complaint as an isolated event.
