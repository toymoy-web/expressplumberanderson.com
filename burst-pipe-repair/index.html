What To Do In The First Five Minutes

A burst pipe is one of the few plumbing problems where speed genuinely limits damage. Shutting off the main water valve, usually near where the line enters the house or in the basement, stops the flow immediately while a technician is en route, which can be the difference between a wet floor and a flooded finished basement.

In Anderson, the most common cause is a hard freeze reaching an exposed or poorly insulated section of pipe, often in an unheated crawlspace, an exterior wall, or a garage. Water expands as it freezes, and the pipe usually splits along a straight section rather than at a fitting.

Signs & Symptoms

What A Burst Pipe Looks Like Before It's Obvious

No Water From A Fixture, Then A Flood

A frozen section can burst quietly before thawing releases the flood.

Water Stains Spreading On A Ceiling

A slow burst inside a wall or floor cavity can show up as staining first.

A Hissing Or Rushing Sound In A Wall

Pressurized water escaping a split pipe often makes an audible sound.

Unexplained Puddling Near An Exterior Wall

A common location for freeze-related breaks in unheated spaces.

Repair

From Shutoff To Repaired Pipe

We locate the exact break, which is sometimes not where the water is visibly pooling since it can travel along a joist or wall cavity before showing up. The damaged section is cut out and replaced with fittings and pipe rated to the Indiana Plumbing Code (675 IAC 16) standard for the material in use, whether that's copper, PEX, or CPVC. We also check nearby exposed sections for the same freeze exposure that caused the original break, since one frozen section often means others nearby are at similar risk.

Before We Arrive

  • Shut off the main water valve if you haven't already.
  • Switch off electricity to any outlet or fixture near standing water.
  • Move furniture, electronics, and rugs away from the wet area.
  • Photograph the damage before cleanup, for insurance documentation.
Prevention

Reducing The Risk Before The Next Freeze

Pipe insulation sleeves on exposed runs in a crawlspace or garage cost very little compared to a single emergency repair call, and are the single most effective prevention step for the freeze pattern we see most often in Anderson. Letting a faucet drip slightly during a hard freeze also relieves pressure inside the line, which reduces the chance of a split even in a marginally insulated section.

Sealing obvious drafts around a crawlspace vent or a foundation penetration where a pipe passes through the wall closes off the specific cold-air path that usually causes a freeze in the first place, rather than just insulating the pipe itself after the fact.
