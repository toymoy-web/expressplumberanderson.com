When Repair Stops Making Sense

Most tank water heaters in Anderson last 10 to 12 years before internal corrosion outpaces what a repair can fix. Anderson's municipal supply is drawn from groundwater through two well fields and two treatment plants operated by Anderson Water Department, and central Indiana groundwater is commonly hard enough that mineral scale builds up inside a tank faster than it would on a soft-water system.

That scale insulates the heating element from the water around it, so the unit runs longer to hit the same temperature and the tank itself corrodes from the inside out. Once a tank is leaking at the seam or the anode rod has been consumed, replacement is the honest recommendation over another round of repairs.

Signs & Symptoms

Signs You Need Replacement, Not Another Repair

No Hot Water

Rusty Or Metallic-Tasting Water

Discolored hot water often points to internal tank corrosion, not the supply line.

Sediment settled at the bottom of the tank rumbling as it heats.

A tank data plate over a decade old is past the point where repair reliably pays off.

Permits & Code

What A Compliant Install Actually Involves

A water heater replacement in Anderson is more than swapping the tank. Gas units need combustion air and venting clearances sized to the Indiana Plumbing Code (675 IAC 16) and Indiana Fuel Gas Code (675 IAC 25) standard, which the state administers through the Indiana Department of Homeland Security. Electric units need the circuit checked against the new unit's draw, not just the old unit's. Indiana requires an IPLA-licensed plumbing contractor for most non-trivial plumbing work, including water heater replacement, and we pull the permit through Madison County's building department as part of the job rather than leaving that step to the homeowner.

We also install a code-required expansion tank on a closed system where the home has a check valve or pressure-reducing valve at the meter, since skipping it is a common cause of a relief valve dripping within weeks of a new install.
