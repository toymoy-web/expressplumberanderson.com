A Distinct Diagnostic Problem

Why Slab Leaks Get Their Own Approach

A slab leak is a leak in a water line running underneath a concrete foundation, which is a genuinely different diagnostic and repair problem from a leak in an accessible wall or crawlspace. The line can't be seen or easily accessed, so the leak has to be located electronically before any concrete comes up, and the repair itself sometimes means rerouting the line entirely rather than breaking into the slab.

A warm spot on an otherwise cool floor, especially over a hot water line, is one of the more reliable early signs, along with a water bill that has risen without an explanation and a water meter that keeps moving with every fixture in the house shut off.

Detection

Thermal Imaging And Acoustic Tracing

Thermal imaging shows the heat signature of a hot water line leaking beneath a slab, since the escaping water warms the concrete directly above the break. Acoustic equipment listens for the sound of pressurized water escaping, which travels through the slab and can be traced to within a foot or two of the actual leak location.

Once located, repair options include a direct spot repair through a small section of concrete, or rerouting the line entirely through the attic or an accessible wall using pipe and fittings sized to the Indiana Plumbing Code (675 IAC 16) standard, which avoids breaking the slab at all. We walk through both with real cost and disruption trade-offs before you choose.

Insurance Note

What Coverage Usually Looks Like

Many homeowner policies cover the cost to access and repair a slab leak itself, since it's typically treated as sudden and accidental damage, though coverage details vary meaningfully by policy and insurer. We provide a written diagnostic report with the confirmed leak location and cause, which most insurers want before approving repair costs.

Flooring replacement over the repaired section, tile, hardwood, or carpet, is usually a separate claim item from the plumbing repair itself, so keeping receipts and photos of both the original flooring and the repair area helps if a claim covers both.
