Seeing Before Guessing

Why A Camera Inspection Comes Before Repair

A sewer camera inspection feeds a waterproof camera on a flexible cable through the line, showing root intrusion, cracks, bellied sections, and buildup in real time on a monitor. That footage is what turns a repair recommendation from a guess into an evidence-based decision, whether the question is what's causing a recurring clog or whether a home's sewer line is sound before closing on a purchase.

We provide the recorded footage to you afterward, which is useful for getting a second opinion, documenting a pre-purchase inspection finding, or simply having a record of the line's condition for future reference.

Equipment

What The Inspection Shows

The camera head includes an integrated light and, on most equipment, a sonde transmitter that lets us mark the exact depth and location of any problem area from the surface using a locator wand. That location data is what makes a spot repair possible without guessing where to dig.

We narrate what the camera is showing during the inspection and provide the recorded footage afterward, along with a written summary of any findings and their approximate location along the line.

Real Estate Transactions

Using An Inspection In A Home Sale

A standard home inspection typically doesn't include the sewer lateral, which is why a separate camera inspection is worth requesting specifically for any older home, particularly one with mature trees near the line's likely path. Finding a problem before closing gives a buyer a real, informed negotiating position or at minimum an accurate picture of a cost to budget for.

Sellers sometimes commission their own inspection ahead of listing, so a documented clean report can be shared with a buyer's inspector rather than facing an unexpected finding during the buyer's own inspection period.

We keep a copy of the recorded footage and written summary on file as well, so if a question comes up months later during closing or a dispute, the documentation still exists rather than depending on remembering what was said.

A commercial property changing tenants is another common trigger for a camera inspection, since an incoming tenant, particularly a restaurant, often wants documented confirmation of the line's condition before signing a lease that makes them responsible for plumbing repairs.
