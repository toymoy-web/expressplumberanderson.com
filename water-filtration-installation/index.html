A Different Job Than Softening

Filtration Vs. Softening: Two Separate Problems

A water softener removes calcium and magnesium through ion exchange, which addresses hardness but does nothing for chlorine taste or odor. Filtration is the separate step that actually removes chlorine and other taste and odor compounds, typically through an activated carbon stage. Confusing the two, or assuming one system handles both jobs, is a common and understandable mix-up we clear up on nearly every water treatment call.

Anderson Water Department treats municipal supply with chlorine as required for safe disinfection, and while that's a normal and necessary part of municipal water treatment, some households prefer to filter it out at the point of entry for taste and odor reasons.

System Types

Carbon Filtration And Point-Of-Entry Systems

A whole-home carbon filtration system installs at the point where water enters the house, treating everything downstream rather than a single faucet. This differs from an under-sink filter, which only treats water at one fixture and leaves the rest of the house, including shower water, untreated.

For households wanting both softening and filtration, the two systems install in sequence, typically filtration first to protect the softener's resin bed from chlorine degradation, then softening afterward. We size and sequence both based on household water use and the results of an on-site water test rather than a generic combination package.

Standards

What Governs A Filtration Installation

Point-of-entry filtration and softener installations both follow the Indiana Plumbing Code standard (675 IAC 16) for backflow protection and drain connections, the same as any other fixture tied into the home's water supply. A bypass valve is required on any point-of-entry system so water can be routed around it for servicing without shutting off the whole house.

We also test water before and after installation to confirm the system is actually performing as expected, rather than assuming a new system is working correctly without verifying it.

Households on private wells rather than Anderson Utilities' municipal supply sometimes need an additional sediment pre-filter stage before the carbon filtration itself, since well water can carry more particulate matter than treated municipal water. We evaluate the incoming water quality before finalizing a system design rather than assuming municipal-supply specs apply universally.
