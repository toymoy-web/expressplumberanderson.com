The Plumbing Behind The Fixture

Why The Rough-In Work Matters As Much As The Fixture

A shower or tub installation is as much about the plumbing behind the wall as the visible fixture itself: the drain, the supply lines, and the valve body all need to be correctly positioned and pressure-tested before any tile or surround goes up. Getting that rough-in wrong is expensive to fix later, since it means opening a finished wall again.

A tub-to-shower conversion is a common request in Anderson's older housing stock, where a full-size tub sometimes no longer fits the household's needs, and that conversion usually involves relocating the drain and reworking the valve rough-in rather than just swapping the visible fixture.

Installation

Rough-In, Set, And Pressure Test

Installation starts with the rough-in: positioning the drain and supply lines to match the new fixture's specifications, which sometimes differ meaningfully from what's already in the wall on an older home, following the Indiana Plumbing Code standard (675 IAC 16) for trap and vent placement. Once the rough-in is set and pressure-tested, the fixture itself is installed and connected, then tested again for leaks at every joint before we consider the job finished.

For a tub-to-shower conversion, the drain location often needs to move, which is more involved than a straight fixture swap and is worth discussing upfront so the quote reflects the actual scope.

Timing With Other Work

What To Address At The Same Time

Opening a bathroom wall for a fixture replacement is a practical time to address anything else nearby that's aging out, an old shutoff valve, a supply line showing corrosion, or a drain trap that's due for replacement. Access is already open, and doing it separately later means opening the wall again.

We flag anything worth addressing during the initial walkthrough rather than discovering it mid-installation and asking you to decide on the spot.

A glass shower door or enclosure is typically a separate line item from the plumbing work itself, coordinated with a glass installer once the shower base and surround are in place, so it's worth budgeting for that separately from the plumbing quote.
