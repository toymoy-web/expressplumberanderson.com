Local Signal

Plumbing In A Gas-Belt River City

Anderson grew up on natural gas and manufacturing along the White River starting in the 1880s. A lot of that era's housing is still standing today. Local plumbers in Madison County routinely flag cast iron and galvanized steel supply lines as common finds in the city's older neighborhoods near downtown and the river corridor. That's a real, checkable fact, not a generic "older homes" line. Galvanized pipe corrodes from the inside out. Over decades, that narrows the interior diameter until water pressure drops and small leaks start showing up at joints.

That combination, an aging housing stock plus genuinely cold Indiana winters, is why burst pipe calls and water heater failures both spike here between December and February. If your home was built before the 1970s and still has its original supply lines, a straightforward inspection can help. It can catch a failing section before it becomes a middle-of-the-night emergency call.

Before You Call

What To Check In A Plumbing Emergency

If you have an active leak or burst pipe:

  • Shut off the main water valve first, then any fixture-specific shutoff if you can reach it safely.
  • Switch off the water heater's power or gas supply if the leak is near the tank.
  • Move electronics, rugs, and furniture away from standing water.
  • Photograph visible damage before cleanup, in case you need it for an insurance claim.
  • Call our 24/7 line so a technician can start diagnosing while you finish securing the area.
