A Small Town With Its Own Water Utility

Chesterfield Runs Its Own Groundwater System

Chesterfield, originally platted as "West Union" in 1830 and renamed four years later, is a small town of roughly 2,500 people with its own municipal water utility, the Chesterfield Water Department. It draws from groundwater and serves about 2,800 people. That's a separate system from Anderson Utilities, though it draws on the same regional groundwater that runs hard across central Indiana.

Being a small, tightly built town means plumbing issues here tend to be concentrated in a compact footprint. A service line or main issue can affect a noticeable share of the town at once compared to a spread-out suburb.

Chesterfield was incorporated in 1858. It straddles the line between Union Township in Madison County and Salem Township in Delaware County, part of the same Indianapolis-Carmel-Anderson metro area as the rest of our service area. Its modest, mostly mid-century housing stock sits close enough to Anderson that many households share commuting and shopping patterns with the larger city next door.
