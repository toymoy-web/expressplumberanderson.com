Built During The Gas Boom

Elwood's Housing Dates To The 1880s Glass Era

Elwood's glass manufacturing boom, sparked by the 1880s discovery of natural gas in the area, is the same industrial story that shaped Anderson. It left Elwood with one of the oldest housing stocks in the region. NeighborhoodScout data shows much of Elwood's housing was built prior to World War II, older on average than Anderson's own housing stock.

That combination, genuinely old pipe plus Indiana's real winter freeze risk, is why Elwood sees a disproportionate share of frozen and burst pipe calls each winter relative to its size. Original galvanized and cast iron lines here have had more decades to corrode than almost anywhere else we service.

Elwood was founded in 1853 and incorporated in 1891. The city still marks its industrial roots with an annual Glass Festival, and Red Gold, a major tomato products employer, remains headquartered in town. The Downtown Historic District preserves the commercial buildings from that boom era, and a lot of the residential blocks around it date from the very same years.
