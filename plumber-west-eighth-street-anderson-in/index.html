About The District

West Eighth Street Historic District

The West Eighth Street district runs along the near-downtown streets of Seventh, Eighth and Ninth. The city describes it as bordered roughly by Jackson Street on the east and Henry Street on the west, and it covers about 100 acres. The National Register listed it in August 1976, and the city designated it a local district in August 1981.

Most of it was built in the 1890s and 1900s, during and just after the gas boom, and many of the homes were occupied by Anderson's business, civic and government leaders. The district holds about 200 structures. They include houses, churches, parks and a few commercial and public buildings.

The architecture is varied: cottages in several styles, Queen Anne, Free Classic, Colonial Revival and a notable group of Italianate homes. Funk Park, on Eighth Street between Chase and Lincoln Streets, has a large gazebo, a brick path and a fountain.

Plumbing For Larger Older Homes

Size Changes The Questions

Homes built for prosperous households were often large, with several floors and more than one bathroom. That changes the plumbing questions. Longer pipe runs, more fixtures and bigger hot water demand all matter more in a big house than in a small cottage.

This is general guidance, not a claim about any one address. A house that has had bathrooms added over the years can have supply and drain lines from different eras, so a repair in one area does not always say much about the rest.

Hot Water Capacity

Several bathrooms can strain one water heater. Sizing a replacement to real household use avoids cold showers.

Multiple Bathrooms

Each added bathroom is another set of supply and drain connections worth checking during an inspection.

Long Drain Runs

Large houses can have long drain paths. A camera inspection shows where a recurring clog actually starts.

Churches And Public Buildings

Larger buildings in the district can need commercial-style plumbing support, including backflow testing.
