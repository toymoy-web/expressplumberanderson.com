More Than A Clog

The Full Range Of Toilet Problems We Handle

Toilet calls cover more ground than a clog: a running toilet that won't shut off wastes water continuously and usually points to a worn flapper or fill valve, while a toilet rocking slightly at the base or leaking at the floor often means the wax ring seal has failed. Both are common, straightforward repairs on most toilets.

A cracked tank or bowl, or a toilet original to a home built decades ago that uses far more water per flush than a modern unit, is often a better candidate for full replacement than continued repair.

Repair Or Replace

Making The Honest Call

A running toilet or a slow fill is almost always a repair, not a replacement, and usually costs far less to fix than to ignore given how much water a continuously running toilet wastes over a billing cycle. A wax ring failure at the base is also a repair in most cases, requiring the toilet to be pulled, a new wax ring set, and the toilet reseated and tested.

Full replacement makes more sense for a cracked tank or bowl, an older high-flow toilet using well over a modern unit's water-per-flush rating, or a toilet that has needed repeated repairs.

What To Do If A Toilet Overflows

  • Lift the tank lid and push the flapper down, or close the fill valve, to stop the water.
  • Turn the shutoff valve behind the toilet clockwise to cut supply entirely.
  • Keep children away from the area until the floor is dry and any spill is cleaned up.
Water Savings

What A Running Toilet Actually Costs

A toilet running continuously can waste hundreds of gallons a day depending on how badly the flapper seals, which shows up as a noticeably higher water bill well before most homeowners identify the actual cause. Since Anderson bills water usage the same as most municipal utilities, a running toilet is one of the cheapest fixes on this list relative to what it saves over even a single billing cycle.

A toilet that flushes weakly but doesn't run continuously is a different problem, usually a partially clogged trapway, mineral buildup restricting the rim jets, or a flush valve that isn't lifting fully. Checking which of those is the actual cause takes a few minutes on-site and changes whether the fix is a $20 part or a full trapway clearing.
