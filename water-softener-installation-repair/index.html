Why It Matters Here

Anderson's Groundwater And Your Plumbing

Anderson Water Department draws the city's supply from groundwater through two well fields and two treatment plants, serving over 21,000 connections citywide. Groundwater across central Indiana is routinely on the hard side of the scale, which is why scale buildup on fixtures, shortened water heater life, and soap that won't lather easily are common complaints we hear on service calls here.

A correctly sized softener addresses all three at once by exchanging the calcium and magnesium in the water for sodium or potassium ions before it ever reaches your fixtures, which is a different job from a carbon filter and won't remove chlorine taste on its own.

Installation

Sizing A System For Your Household

Softener sizing depends on household size, daily water use, and the actual hardness of your specific supply, not a one-size-fits-all unit. We test your water on-site before recommending a grain capacity, since guessing high wastes salt and guessing low means the system runs out of capacity mid-week.

For repairs, the most common failure point is the control valve or the resin bed losing capacity after years of use, both of which are usually fixable without replacing the whole system.

Maintenance

What Keeps A Softener Working Correctly

A softener that runs low on salt stops treating water even though it keeps cycling normally, which is the most common reason a system that was working fine suddenly lets hard water through again. Checking the brine tank monthly and refilling before it runs empty is the single biggest factor in a softener actually delivering the soft water it was sized to provide. Installation itself, particularly the required air gap and backflow protection on the drain line, follows the same Indiana Plumbing Code standard (675 IAC 16) framework as any other fixture tied into the home's supply.

Beyond salt level, the resin bed inside most residential softeners lasts 10 to 15 years before it loses exchange capacity permanently, at which point a repair stops being cost-effective and the resin tank itself needs replacing rather than just the control valve.

A softener installed just after moving into an older Anderson home is also the point where we often find the incoming shutoff valve itself is original and seized, which is worth replacing at the same time rather than as a separate emergency call later.
